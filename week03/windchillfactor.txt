wind chill factor: what is it?

Wind chill factor is the measure of how cold it feels outside when wind is combined with low air temperatures, 
not just the actual temperature itself. It represents the accelerated heat loss from exposed skin due to moving 
air, which can make conditions far more dangerous than the thermometer alone suggests.

🌬️ What Wind Chill Really Means
Definition: Wind chill (or wind chill factor/index) is the apparent temperature felt on exposed skin due to the 
combined effect of air temperature and wind speed.

Key Point: It applies only to people and animals, not objects. Pipes, cars, or radiators cannot cool below the actual 
air temperature, though wind may make them reach that temperature faster.

🔢 How It’s Calculated
The National Weather Service (NWS) formula (used in the U.S. and Canada) is:

Wind Chill (°F) = 35.74 + 0.6215𝑇 − 35.75(𝑉**0.16) + 0.4275𝑇(𝑉**0.16)
T = Air temperature (°F)
V = Wind speed (mph)

Conditions:
Only valid for temperatures ≤ 50°F (10°C) and wind speeds > 3 mph (5 km/h).

Wind Chill Factor – Key Points

Wind chill factor is the apparent temperature felt on exposed skin when wind combines with cold air.

It applies only to humans and animals, not objects.

Formula (North American standard):
WCI = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75(V^0.16) + 0.4275T(V^0.16)
where T = temperature in °F, V = wind speed in mph.

Valid only when temperature ≤ 10°C (50°F) and wind speed > 4.8 km/h (3 mph).

Conversions:
°C ↔ °F: (°C × 9/5) + 32, (°F − 32) × 5/9
km/h ↔ mph: mph = km/h ÷ 1.609

Health risks: frostbite and hypothermia increase as wind chill drops.
